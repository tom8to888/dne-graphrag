Welcome to the 99th issue of the World Tennis
Coaching & Sport Science Review, the second of 2026,
celebrating 34 years of publication. This is a special
Issue, dedicated to the theme “Wheelchair Tennis.”
Through this edition, we aim to showcase the latest
advancements on this modality and how different
initiatives are shaping the future of wheelchair tennis
globally across all ages and abilities.
In this introductory editorial we share several
important news that have occurred since our last issue.
World Tennis. The new name for the
International Tennis Federation (ITF)
On 25 June 2026, the International Tennis Federation,
the organisation that has governed tennis on a
global basis since 1913, become World Tennis, and
announced its vision for the long-term sustainable
growth of the sport worldwide.
The transformation to World Tennis marks the start
of a new era for the organisation. At the heart of its
ambition for the future success of one of the world's
most popular sport, World Tennis is committing to
unlocking a ‘world of opportunities’ for people of all
abilities, ages and backgrounds – both on and off the
court.
World Tennis has outlined five strategic priorities to
achieve its ambition for tennis:
•	 Growing participation: Connect and align one
of the largest networks of national associations
across world sport to increase the number of
recreational players and enable more people of all
ages and abilities to enjoy the proven health and
social benefits of tennis.
•	 Powering
future
stars:
Deliver
further
enhancements to the World Tennis global
player pathway – the main talent pipeline to
the professional game – build new markets and
provide more competitive opportunities all over
the world.

World Tennis also revealed its goal for the sport to
achieve ambitious new levels of global participation,
targeting strong growth in the numbers playing tennis
worldwide from 106 million players to 140 million by
2035 – an increase of more than 30 percent.
World Tennis has also launched the first-ever Global
Tennis Fan Survey – an open invitation to fans and
tennis communities in every country to share their
views and ensure their voices are heard as the sport
evolves in the years ahead.
Important news regarding Luca Santilli
Luca Santilli, a long-standing and much valued
member of World Tennis senior leadership team, after
35 years dedicated to growing tennis globally including
his significant contributions at World Tennis, decided
to retire from World Tennis to enjoy more family time
and pursue new ventures.
Luca’s journey in tennis began in 1991. He joined
World Tennis from FITP in 1999, and his impact has
been immense. Luca has travelled to over 100 countries
during his time at the World Tennis, championing
development and education programmes. Under his
leadership, World Tennis strengthened its unique
global development structure, placing World Tennis
people and resources closer to grassroots tennis
across all regions in close collaboration with National
and Regional Tennis Associations.
Reflecting on his career, Luca shares this message with
us all: “My career dedicated to growing tennis—World
Tennis included—spans 35 years, and it’s not over yet.
Today, I’ve decided to retire from a full-time role, but I
will continue my tennis and sports activity in new and
different capacities. I intend to spend more time with
my family and, injuries permitting, get my tennis back
on track. I’ll also pursue my love of sailing, training
for a voyage around the Mediterranean in 2027. I’ll
divide my time between my family home in the UK and
my native Italy. My heartfelt thanks to everyone I’ve
been fortunate enough to collaborate with these past
decades.”
Luca left World Tennis at the end of May. World Tennis
has already identified a successor to take over Luca’s
responsibilities, ensuring continuity and building on
the strong foundation he has established.
Please join us in thanking Luca for his incredible
contribution and wishing him well in this exciting new
chapter.
2026 World Tennis Regional Coaches
Conferences
In 2026, World Tennis with the support of Olympic
Solidarity, the Regional Associations, and the
member nations, is organizing a series of Regional
Coaches Conferences across various regions, aimed at
enhancing coaching standards and promoting tennis
development worldwide. These conferences will take
place in seven distinct countries with a carefully
curated programme tailored to the specific needs
and contexts of the region. This initiative continues
the World Tennis commitment to advancing tennis
education and professional development, following
the success of previous Regional Coaches Conferences,
which have proven instrumental in boosting the
knowledge and skills of coaches globally.
The
WT/CAT
East
African
Regional
Coaches
Conference will be hosted in Nairobi (Kenya), from
28 to 30 September 2026. Confirmed speakers so far
include Jürg Buehler (SUI), Kris Soutar (UK), Mathieu
Wagner (FRA), Edita Liachoviciute (LTU), Rosemary
Owino (KEN), Dina Razafimahatratra (MAD), Francis
Rogoi (KEN), George Oyoo (KEN), Riaan Kruger (WT)
and Thierry Ntwali (WT).
The WT/ATF West & Central Asian Regional Coaches
Conference will be hosted in Almaty, Kazakhstan at
the Tennis club “CSKA” from 17 to 19 October 2026.
Confirmed speakers include Philipp Born (GER),
Ales Filipcic (SLO), Simone Bertino (ITA), Irena
Chichmarova (BYS), Yaroslava Shvedova (KAZ), Marco
Panichi (ITA), Farid Yazdanyar (IRA), Riaan Kruger
(WT) and Amir Borghei (WT).
The WT/COTECC Central American and Caribbean
Regional Coaches Conference will be hosted in
Panama City, from 30 October to 1 November 2026.
Confirmed speakers include Jason Allen (USA), MarieFrance Mercier (CAN), Valeria MacLoughlin (ARG),
Ernest Baiget (ESP), Oderaiza Soto (PUR), Carolina
Vera (SUR), Kyle Hannays (TTO), Ismar Amaya (GUA),

The WT/ATF South, Southeast & East Asian Regional
Coaches Conference will be hosted in Hong Kong at the

(NED), Petra Russeger (AUT), Beti Sekulovski (AUS),
Roland Kraut (PHI), Gary Purcell (WT), Jonathan

The Tennis Europe Coaches Conference will take place
in Antalya, Turkey from 6-7 November 2026 featuring
confirmed speakers to date such as Michelangelo
Dell’Edera (ITA), Luigi Bertino (ITA), Jürg Bühler (SUI),
Olha Lazarchuk (WT), Tim Jones (WT), and Miguel

The WT/CAT Northern African Coaches Conference

2026. Confirmed speakers to date include Emily

These conferences will explore a broad spectrum
of subjects relevant to tennis coaching, coach
education, gender equality, and development, with
a strong focus on exchanging ideas and fostering
international cooperation. Participants can expect
detailed presentations covering areas such as physical
conditioning,
technique,
tactics,
best
practice
scenarios, mental training, modern pedagogical
trends, player development pathways, and examples
of mass participation programmes. These themes have
consistently formed the foundation of previous World
Tennis Coaches Conferences, which have welcomed
renowned keynote speakers and engaged thousands
of participants through both in-person and online
formats. This continued level of interest highlights
the significant role these conferences play within the
World Tennis Coach Education Programme.
For individuals who are unable to participate in
these conferences on site, the World Tennis Academy
provides on-demand access to recordings from every
session. This opportunity enables a wider community
of coaches, tennis professionals, and sports enthusiasts
to benefit from the knowledge and experience shared
by leading experts. To gain access to these resources,
users can create an account at www.itf-academy.com
and enrol in the relevant conference. Through this
approach, the educational value of the conferences
remains available to the global tennis community
well beyond the live events. Additional information
on registration procedures and conference package
bookings is available through the World Tennis
Academy here: www.itf-academy.com
