Welcome to this special issue of the World Tennis
Coaching & Sport Science Review, marking fifty years
since the emergence of wheelchair tennis. The articles
in this special edition demonstrate how far wheelchair
tennis has progressed and where it continues to
evolve. Contributions span the athlete pathway,
from participation and talent development to highperformance preparation, coaching, classification,
equipment, biomechanics, performance analysis and
interdisciplinary sport science.
Several papers challenge traditional approaches
to athlete development, highlighting the need for
inclusive and evidence-informed pathways that
reflect the diverse experiences of athletes with
disabilities. These journeys are influenced by factors
such as impairment, access to equipment, coaching,
classification
competition
opportunities.
Contributions on learning design and representative
practice
also
encourage
coaches
to
create
environments that better reflect match-play demands.
Coaching remains central to the sport’s development.
This edition highlights the importance of disabilityspecific knowledge, communication, mentorship,
coach education and athlete-centred practice, while
identifying the need to better support coaches
responsible for introducing, retaining and developing
players.
Science, Innovation and the Modern
Game
Research in wheelchair tennis has grown considerably
over the past two decades, with the number of
published studies increasing substantially in the last
decade alone. Early research focused on understanding
the sport's unique coaching and performance demands
(Sanz Rivas, 2003), while more recent work has
explored topics including match analysis (SánchezPay et al., 2015), wheelchair configuration and mobility
performance (Rietveld et al., 2021), psychosocial
outcomes (Richardson et al., 2015), and strength and
conditioning considerations for elite players (Roetert
et al., 2023). Together, these studies demonstrate the

The growth of wheelchair tennis as a research field
is also reflected in the increasing number of doctoral
theses completed worldwide. Research has examined
coaching behaviour and match demands in Spain
(Sanz Rivas, 2003; Sánchez Pay, 2015), wheelchair
biomechanics in France (Bascou, 2012), wheeling

United Kingdom (Mason, 2011; Sindall, 2016; D'Angeli,

work has focused on wheelchair tennis-specific agility
and hitting accuracy testing (Enquist, 2022).
Collectively, this body of work highlights growing
international interest in wheelchair tennis as a context
for research in coaching, biomechanics, physiology,
technology and performance science. Importantly,
wheelchair tennis research has not only focused on
performance enhancement but has also demonstrated
the broader value of participation. Richardson et
al. (2015) highlighted the psychosocial benefits of
wheelchair tennis for players in developing nations,
